Who's Cooking

About Vince

Vince grew up watching his Vietnamese family cook with soul — big pots, loud flavors, everybody eating. He carried that into 15+ years of feeding firefighter crews who needed real food after real calls.

Diesel Delights is where the family table meets the firehouse stove: signature plates cooked Vince's way, every single time. No copies. No shortcuts. Just food that hits like home.

Before the restaurant, there was the food trailer — and before that, years on the line at Wayside Noodles, Pete Teriyaki, and every hustle in between. All of it sharpened the instinct that shows up on every plate.

Diesel Fish Sauce

Clear, sharp, all-purpose nuoc cham-style sauce. Tangy, slightly sweet, with garlic bite and pepper finish. The base layer of the Diesel flavor system.

Diesel Peanut Sauce

Rich. Nutty. Savory.

Fish-sauce based peanut dip — not sweet like Thai peanut sauce. Savory, nutty, with body from the oil and depth from the fish sauce. Chunky peanut texture.

  • Fish Sauce

The Journey

Vince's Road

The Family Kitchen

Grew up in a Vietnamese household where the stove was always on. Fish sauce, lemongrass, and charred meats were the background of every day. Absorbed it all before he ever picked up a knife himself.

The Restaurant Grind

Ran the line at Wayside Noodles, Pete Teriyaki, and every hustle in between. Learned how to feed a neighborhood every night and still keep the food honest.

The Firehouse

15+ years cooking for brothers and sisters in the fire service. No room for weak plates. That pressure shaped everything: big flavors, hot plates, real portions, and the kind of care that says “I got you.”

The Trailer

First time Vince put his own name behind his own food. The “Vince's way” signatures started here: Vietnamese roots with a chef's touch, comfort food with an edge.

Diesel Delights

The brick-and-mortar chapter. Every dish is a signature or it doesn't make the menu. This is where the family table, the firehouse stove, and the trailer hustle all collide into one kitchen.
