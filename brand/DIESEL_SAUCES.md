## 1. DIESEL CHILI SAUCE (DC)

**Bright, fresh, spicy.** Fish-sauce based green chili sauce with cilantro
and lime that cuts through rich, fatty, or smoky proteins.

**Paired with:** Red Pure Diesel Leaf, Diesel Torch Sensation

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount (small batch) | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|-------|
| Fish sauce | 3 tbsp | The salty backbone |
| Lime juice | 2 tbsp | Fresh squeezed only |
| Sriracha | 1 – 2 tbsp | Adjust heat to your level |
| Cilantro, chopped fine | 3 – 4 tbsp (a good handful) | Stems too — that's where the flavor is |
| Green onion, chopped fine | 2 – 3 stalks | White and green parts |
| Sugar | 1 tsp | Just enough to round the edges |
| Water | 1 – 2 tbsp | Only if too intense, thin slightly |

### Method
1. Chop cilantro and green onion fine. Put in a bowl.
2. Add fish sauce, lime juice, sriracha, and sugar. Stir until sugar
   dissolves.
3. Taste. It should hit: salty first, then sour, then fresh herb, then
   the sriracha heat on the finish.
4. Add water only if it's too concentrated for dipping.
5. Let sit 5–10 minutes so the herbs release into the liquid.

### Vince's Dial
- Heavy on the cilantro and green onion — this is a GREEN sauce, not
  a clear one. You should see the herbs floating.
- The lime has to be fresh. Bottled lime juice tastes flat here.
- Make it same-day. This sauce dies overnight — the herbs go dull.

### Ratio to Remember
**3 parts fish sauce : 2 parts lime : 1–2 parts sriracha : herbs heavy**

## 2. DIESEL FISH SAUCE

**Clear, sharp, all-purpose.** A clean nuoc cham-style dipping and
dressing sauce that works as the base layer under the ginger chicken.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount (small batch) | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|-------|
| Vinegar (rice vinegar) | 3 tbsp | Rice vinegar keeps it clean |
| Water | 1/4 cup | Dilutes to dipping strength |
| Sugar | 2 tbsp | Dissolve fully |
| Salt | 1/2 tsp | Taste first — may not need much |
| Garlic, minced fine | 2 – 3 cloves | |
| Black pepper | 1/2 tsp | Fresh cracked is better |

### Method
1. Warm the water slightly (microwave 20 seconds or warm tap water).
2. Dissolve sugar and salt in the warm water. Stir until clear.
3. Add vinegar, garlic, and pepper. Stir.
4. Taste. Should be: tangy, slightly sweet, with garlic bite and pepper
   on the finish.

### Vince's Dial
- This one is deliberately simple. Don't overthink it.
- The garlic should be minced fine enough that it distributes evenly
  in every spoonful.
- Keeps refrigerated 5–7 days.

### Ratio to Remember
**1 part vinegar : ~1.5 parts water : sugar + salt to balance : garlic + pepper**

## 3. DIESEL FISH-GARLIC SAUCE

**The Ginger Chicken's partner.** A heavier, more aggressive version of
the fish sauce — loaded with garlic. This is specifically for Diesel Cure.

**Paired with:** Diesel Cure (Ginger Chicken)

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount (small batch) | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|-------|
| Fish sauce | 3 tbsp | Good quality — Red Boat or Viet Huong |
| Garlic, minced or pressed | 5 – 6 cloves | TONS. This is the point. |
| Black pepper | 1 tsp | Heavy |
| Sugar | 1 – 2 tbsp | Balances the fish sauce punch |
| Salt | Pinch | Only if needed after tasting |
| Water | 2 – 3 tbsp | Thins to drizzle consistency |

### Method
1. Dissolve sugar in warm water.
2. Add fish sauce, garlic, pepper, and salt. Stir.
3. Taste. It should be: aggressively garlicky, salty-sweet, with pepper
   heat. This is not subtle — it's meant to punch through hot rice and
   ginger chicken.
4. Let the garlic sit in the sauce at least 10 minutes before serving
   so it infuses.

### Vince's Dial
- "Tons of garlic" means tons of garlic. If you're not pressing 5–6
  cloves minimum for a small batch, add more.
- The pepper matters here. It's the secondary heat behind the garlic.
- This sauce should make the ginger chicken plate smell incredible from
  three feet away.

### Ratio to Remember
**Fish sauce + way more garlic than you think + pepper + sugar to balance**

## 4. DIESEL PEANUT SAUCE

**Rich, nutty, savory.** Fish-sauce based peanut dip — not sweet like
Thai peanut sauce. This one has body and salt.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount (small batch) | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|-------|
| Peanuts (roasted, unsalted) or peanut butter | 1/4 cup peanuts or 3 tbsp PB | If whole peanuts: crush or blend |
| Fish sauce | 1 – 2 tbsp | Savory depth |
| Neutral oil (canola or peanut oil) | 1 tbsp | Body and smoothness |
| Sugar | 1 – 2 tsp | Just enough to round it |
| Salt | Pinch | Taste after fish sauce — may not need |
| Water | 2 – 3 tbsp | Thin to dipping consistency |

### Method
1. If using whole peanuts: crush in a mortar or pulse in a blender
   with the oil until semi-smooth (some chunks are good).
   If using peanut butter: skip this step.
2. Add fish sauce, sugar, salt, and water. Stir until combined.
3. Taste. Should be: nutty first, then savory from the fish sauce,
   with a gentle sweetness. Not a sweet sauce — a savory one.
4. Adjust water for thickness. Should coat a spring roll but still
   flow off a spoon.

### Vince's Dial
- Fish sauce is the move that makes this different from every other
  peanut sauce. It adds umami depth that sugar-heavy peanut sauces
  don't have.
- Keep some peanut chunks in there. Texture matters.
- Optional: add a squeeze of lime or a pinch of chili flakes at
  service for an extra layer.

### Ratio to Remember
**Peanuts + fish sauce for salt + oil for body + sugar just to round**

## 5. DIESEL HOISIN SAUCE (DH)

**Sweet-savory glaze/dip.** Vince's clean hoisin built from simple
ingredients — vinegar, garlic, water, salt, sugar. Not a thick, dark,
store-bought hoisin. This is lighter and brighter.

**Paired with:** Diesel Counter Health (Spring Rolls)

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount (small batch) | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|-------|
| Vinegar (rice vinegar) | 2 tbsp | Brightness |
| Garlic, minced fine | 3 – 4 cloves | |
| Water | 1/4 cup | Base liquid |
| Salt | 1/2 tsp | |
| Sugar | 2 – 3 tbsp | This sauce leans sweeter than the others |
| Soy sauce (optional) | 1 tbsp | Adds color and depth if you want |

### Method
1. Warm water slightly. Dissolve sugar and salt.
2. Add vinegar and garlic. Stir.
3. Optional: add soy sauce for darker color and more savory depth.
4. Taste. Should be: sweet-tangy with garlic presence. Think of it
   as the friendly, dippable sauce — not aggressive like the DC or
   the fish-garlic.

### Vince's Dial
- This is your "everybody likes it" sauce. Keep it approachable.
- If you want it thicker/more glaze-like, reduce slightly on low heat
  for 3–4 minutes, or add a tiny cornstarch slurry (1/2 tsp in cold
  water, stir in while warm).
- The garlic should be minced fine enough that it doesn't overpower —
  this one is about balance, not aggression.

### Ratio to Remember
**Vinegar + water + sugar (lean sweet) + garlic + salt**

## Sauce Production Schedule

### Made Fresh Daily
- **Diesel Chili Sauce (DC)** — herbs die overnight, make same-day
- **Diesel Fish-Garlic Sauce** — garlic is best fresh

### Made Every 2–3 Days
- **Diesel Fish Sauce** — holds fine refrigerated
- **Diesel Hoisin (DH)** — holds well, refreshes easily
- **Diesel Peanut Sauce** — stir before service, may need a splash of
  water to re-thin

### Prep Tip
Garlic is in 4 out of 5 sauces. Prep all your garlic at once at the
start of each day, then split it across sauce stations. Same with the
sugar-water base — dissolve a bigger batch of sugar water and portion
it out.
