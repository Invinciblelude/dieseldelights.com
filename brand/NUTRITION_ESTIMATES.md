> These are planning-stage estimates based on typical ingredient weights
> and USDA calorie data. **Final numbers should be verified** by weighing
> your actual portions and running them through a calculator.

---

## How to Get Exact Numbers

1. **Weigh every ingredient** in one serving using a kitchen scale (grams).
2. Look up each ingredient at [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov).
3. Multiply: (grams used) × (calories per gram from USDA) = calories from that ingredient.
4. Add all ingredients together = total calories per plate.

**Free tools that do the math for you:**
- [Nutritionix Restaurant Calculator](https://www.nutritionix.com/business/restaurant-nutrition-calculator)
- [MyFitnessPal Recipe Calculator](https://www.myfitnesspal.com)
- [CalorieKing](https://www.calorieking.com)
- [Cronometer](https://cronometer.com) (most detailed micronutrient breakdown)

## How to Verify These Numbers

### Step-by-step (do this once per dish)

1. **Make one plate exactly how you'd serve it.**
2. **Before combining, weigh each ingredient separately on a kitchen
   scale in grams.** Write it down.
3. **Go to [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov)** and
   search each ingredient. Note the calories per 100g.
4. **Math:** (your grams ÷ 100) × calories per 100g = calories from
   that ingredient.
5. **Add all ingredients** = total plate calories.
6. **Record it** so you never have to do it again unless the recipe
   changes.

### Example: Diesel Cure chicken

- Chicken thigh: you used 135g → USDA says 209 cal/100g → 135 ÷ 100 × 209 = **282 cal**
- Rice: you used 190g → USDA says 130 cal/100g → 190 ÷ 100 × 130 = **247 cal**
- Oil: you used 14g → USDA says 884 cal/100g → 14 ÷ 100 × 884 = **124 cal**
- (continue for all ingredients, add up)

### Faster option

Use [Cronometer.com](https://cronometer.com) (free account) — enter
each ingredient and weight, it calculates everything including
micronutrients. Most accurate free tool available.

## Do You Need to Post Calories?

**California law (SB 1420):** Restaurants with **20+ locations** must
post calorie counts on menus. As a single-location restaurant, you are
**not legally required** to post calories.

**But you might want to anyway** for marketing — especially for the
Diesel Counter Health, Diesel Leaf, and Torch Sensation, which are all
under 320 calories. Highlighting those as lighter options brings in
health-conscious customers without changing your menu.
