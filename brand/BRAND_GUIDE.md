# Diesel Delights — Brand Guide

---

## The Name

**Diesel Delights** — bold, memorable, and unapologetically energetic.
"Diesel" carries weight, power, and blue-collar authenticity.
"Delights" softens it with pleasure and warmth.
Together it says: *this food hits hard and makes you happy.*

Full name on signage: **Diesel Delights by Vince**

## Slogan Options

Pick one primary slogan and use the others for merch, social, and interior art.

| Slogan | Use Case |
|--------|----------|
| **"Turn the gas up."** | Primary — signage, menu header, website hero |
| **"Fired up to feed you."** | Friendly/warm — catering cards, to-go bags |
| **"Every plate is a signature."** | Quality promise — menu footer, about page |
| **"We cook till it blows up."** | Hype — merch, social media, kitchen wall |
| **"Vietnamese roots. Firehouse soul."** | Story — press, PR, bio |
| **"Cooked Vince's way."** | Personal — plate stickers, napkins |

### Why Not "Gaslighting"

"Gaslighting" now universally means emotional manipulation. Using it — even
as a flame pun — risks bad press, social media pile-ons, and turning off
families. The alternatives above carry the same fire energy without the
baggage.

## Brand Voice

How Diesel Delights talks — on menus, social media, signage, and to staff:

- **Confident, never arrogant.** "We know this plate is fire" not "we're
  better than everyone."
- **Warm, like a family dinner.** Talk to guests like they're at your house.
- **Direct.** Short sentences. No corporate fluff. Say what it is.
- **A little loud.** Exclamation points are okay. Caps on one word are okay.
  But don't scream everything.
- **Real.** Talk about the food, the process, the hustle. No fake
  marketing-speak.

**Examples:**
- Instagram caption: "Diesel Cure. Ginger chicken. Hot rice. Pickles on
  the side. This plate fixes everything. Pull up."
- Menu description: "Pho Duoi — Ancient Diesel Soup. One deep house broth,
  12+ hours in the pot, tuned up Vince's way."
- Welcome sign: "You're at Vince's table now."
- Reel caption: "Red Pure Diesel Leaf. Tri-tip. Lettuce wrap. Diesel Chili.
  That's all you need to know."

| **Herb Green** | #4A7C59 | Fresh accent — herbs, garnish imagery |
| **Brass Gold** | #C9A84C | Premium touch — Vince Experience, specials |

### Typography Direction

- **Headlines:** Bold, slightly industrial sans-serif (think Bebas Neue,
  Oswald, or Barlow Condensed). All-caps for section headers.
- **Body:** Clean, readable sans-serif (Inter, DM Sans, or similar).
- **Accent:** A hand-drawn or brush script for "by Vince" and signature
  callouts — personal, not generic.

### Photography Style

- Moody, warm lighting. Dark backgrounds, food lit from the side.
- Hands in frame — Vince plating, chopsticks grabbing, steam rising.
- Raw ingredients on butcher paper or dark wood.
- Never sterile or stock-photo clean. Always real, always textured.

### Interior Vibe Direction

- **Industrial-warm:** exposed brick or dark paint, warm Edison-style
  lighting, natural wood tabletops, open kitchen or visible pass.
- **Sound:** upbeat but not deafening — hip-hop, R&B, Vietnamese pop at a
  level where tables can still talk.
- **Kitchen wall art:** the slogan "We cook till it blows up" in bold type
  behind the pass window.
- **One feature wall:** Vince's story in big type or a mural — the family,
  the firehouse, the trailer, the restaurant.

## Target Guest Profiles

### The Natomas Regular
Lives within 3–5 miles. Mixed household income $60–$100K. Eats out 2–4x
a week. Knows Vietnamese food. Wants quality, speed, and value. Will
become a weekly habit if the food stays consistent.

### The Foodie Explorer
Drives from Midtown, East Sac, or Elk Grove for a specific dish they saw
on Instagram. Orders the Diesel Torch Sensation oysters and Pho Duoi
Takes photos. Leaves a Yelp review. Tells friends.

### The Family Table
Parents with kids. Needs approachable items (Ancient Diesel Fried Rice,
Diesel Cure, Counter Health spring rolls). Wants to spend $40–$60 for
the whole family and leave full and happy.

### The Vince Experience Guest
Food-obsessed. Willing to pay $55–$75/person for a set menu night. Wants
the story, the technique, and the interaction with the chef. This is your
future Michelin audience — cultivate them here.
