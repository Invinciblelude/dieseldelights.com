## Full Origin Story

### The Family Table

Vince didn't grow up as "the cook" in a Vietnamese household where somebody
was always chopping, simmering, or grilling. The stove was never off. Fish
sauce, lemongrass, charred meats, and fresh herbs were the background of
every day. As a kid he absorbed all of it without realizing he was being
trained. As an adult, he had to step up and teach his own hands to do what
he'd watched his family do for decades — translating those flavors into
something that was his.

### The Firehouse

For over 15 years Vince cooked for his brothers and sisters in the fire
service. That kitchen has no room for weak plates. After a bad call,
exhausted crews sit down and the food either hits or it doesn't — and they
will absolutely let you know. Vince learned to cook with urgency, honesty,
and volume: big flavors, hot plates, real portions, and the kind of care
that says *I got you* without saying a word. The firehouse taught him that
consistency is love, and love is non-negotiable.

### The Grind

Outside the station and the restaurants, Vince was always moving — running
the line at spots like Wayside Noodles and Pete Teriyaki, working multiple
jobs, taking risks, winning and losing money, and fighting through health
scares and low moments that would've made most people tap out. Through all
of it, three young daughters and a wife holding down the house were the
reason to keep building something bigger. The grind wasn't glamorous, but it
was honest, and it sharpened every instinct Vince brings to the kitchen now.

### The Trailer

Before the restaurant, there was the food trailer — the first time Vince
put his own name behind his own food and served it to strangers. That's
where the "Vince's way" signatures started taking shape: Vietnamese roots
with a chef's touch, comfort food with an edge, plates that people came
back for because they couldn't get them anywhere else. The trailer proved
the concept. It proved the demand. And it proved Vince could build something
from nothing.

### Diesel Delights

Diesel Delights is the brick-and-mortar chapter. A Natomas neighborhood
spot where every dish on the menu is a signature plate or it doesn't make
the menu. Nem rolls, Peking buns, diesel rice plates, loud salads, and
bowls of pho that hit different — all cooked Vince's way, every service,
no exceptions.

This isn't a copy of another Vietnamese restaurant. This isn't a chain
pretending to care. This is one chef's lifetime of family flavors, firehouse
cooking, trailer hustle, and relentless work stacked into a room where
every plate feels like Vince just cooked it for you personally.

The mission is simple: feed people so well they can't stop talking about it,
and build something his daughters can point to and say, *my dad built that.*
